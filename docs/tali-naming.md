# Quarian names and Tali's drone pool

Notes behind `characters/tali/drones.toml`.

## Structure (canon)
A quarian's full name is given name, apostrophe, clan name, then a ship title: "nar" (child of, the birth ship) before Pilgrimage, "vas" (crew of) after. Tali'Zorah nar Rayya became Tali'Zorah vas Neema, then vas Normandy.

## Chiktikka vas Paus
- "Chiktikka" is widely read as a nod to Chiktikka Fastpaws, the raccoon companion of the deity Baervan Wildwanderer from the Forgotten Realms. Bioware's Baldur's Gate games are set there, and the drone's "go for the eyes" bark echoes Minsc from Baldur's Gate.
- "vas Paus" uses the crew-of title, so the drone is crew of a "Paus" that no quarian in the games serves on. Fans also hear it as "Paws", a likely second layer of the Fastpaws joke.
- The name is affectionate: a pet's name with a full crew title, which is how Tali treats her.

## Patterns in canon names
Sample: Tali, Zorah, Rael, Kal, Reegar, Han, Gerrel, Zaal, Koris, Shala, Raan, Daro, Xen, Veetor, Nara, Golo, Mekk, Lia, Vael, Kenn, Prazza. Ships: Rayya, Neema, Tonbay, Moreh, Qwib-Qwib, Idenna, Usela, Alarei, Ulnay. Words: keelah, se'lai, bosh'tet, ke'sed, tasi, Rannoch.

- **Doubled vowels** mark long sounds: Zaal, Raan, Reegar, Veetor, Keelah, Neema.
- **Doubled consonants**: Mekk, Kenn, Rayya, Idenna, Prazza, Chiktikka.
- **Repetition for small or silly things**: Qwib-Qwib, and the chik-tik in Chiktikka.
- **Sounds**: heavy on k, r, l, n, z, x, sh, v and t. P, b and f are rare, which makes "Paus" stand out.
- **Endings**: -ah, -a, -el, -is, -en, -eh, -ay, -or.
- **Length**: two syllables is the norm, three at most.

## How the pool was built
Every drone shares "vas Paus" so the family reads as Chiktikka's line. Each name uses at least one pattern above (a doubled vowel, a doubled consonant, repetition, or a canon ending) and avoids p, b and f. Roles match the jobs agents will do (calendar, email, research), so Tali can say "I'll send Raala" and it means something. Kiri-Kiri and Qwibbit lean on the repetition pattern for the silly ones.
